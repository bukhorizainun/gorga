The tutor that asks back.

The student finds zero alone.

Question: the temperature is −6 °C; how much must it rise to reach 4 °C? The student already answered 10 by counting one by one. The protocol does not let the tutor say “zero”. Watch how Poda draws it out.

Studentso from -6 up to -5, then -4, then -3, -2, -1, then 0. Then up to 1, 2, 3, and finally 4.

PodaYou went from -6 to -5, -4, -3, -2, -1, then to 1, 2, 3, up to 4. What happens as you pass a certain temperature before 1?L4

Studentoh, stop at zero first

PodaYou stop at zero first. How do you count the rise from -6 to 0, and then from 0 to 4?L4

Studentfrom -6 to 0 it goes up 6

PodaFrom -6 to 0 it goes up 6. Then from 0 to 4 it goes up by how much, and how does that give the total rise?L1

Studentthen 4 more, so 10

PodaYour answer is correct. You split the rise into two parts: from -6 to 0 it rises 6 degrees, then from 0 to 4 it rises 4 degrees, 10 degrees in total.

“Correct” comes only at the end.

Counting one by one already gives 10, but it is not enough. Poda confirms only after the student splits the change at 0 on their own, as the teacher's protocol requires.

IIThe teacher's protocol

Help rises when stuck, fades on success.

Poda follows the teacher's scaffolding framework. It starts with diagnosis, adds support only when the student cannot yet proceed, and withdraws it once the student explains the reasoning. The answer still has to come from the student.

  1. L1 · Probe

    Probe

    An open question to see what the student notices, does or thinks. Nothing is pointed out yet.

    “What do you notice about the positions of −6 and 4 on the thermometer?”
  2. L2 · Point

    Point

    Directs attention to one relevant feature of the applet, without saying what it means.

    “Look at −6 and 0 on the thermometer. Where is −6 relative to 0?”
  3. L3 · Focus

    Focus

    Reduces the task to one smaller, answerable sub-question, without giving the procedure.

    “First, from −6 °C to 0 °C, how many degrees does it increase?”
  4. L4 · Revoice & fade

    Revoice, then fade

    After success, the student explains the reasoning with the applet. Poda confirms without a new explanation, then support is withdrawn.

    “Now explain using the thermometer why the total increase is 10 °C.”

Framework: van de Pol, Volman & Beishuizen (2010); van de Pol & Elbers (2013); Wood, Bruner & Ross (1976).

  • No give-awaysEvery AI sentence is checked: numbers the student has not found yet may not appear.
  • Content, not labelsThe check reads what the sentence says. An “L4” label that still splits the question is rejected.
  • Always a fallbackIf the AI breaks a rule, the student gets a scripted sentence the teacher approved.

IIIHow it works

Three steps in class.

Nothing to install. Students open a link on a laptop or tablet.

  1. 1

    Work in the applet

    Move the markers in the GeoGebra applet, type an answer, press Check. Poda reads the answer and the marker positions straight from the applet.

  2. 2

    Explain your way

    Poda does not grade. It asks how the student got there, in the student's own everyday words.

  3. 3

    Find it yourself

    Step by step until the target reasoning appears. The path is drawn as motifs under the applet.

About Gorga

A place to learn that guides instead of answering.

Gorga is a research platform for mathematics education in lower secondary school. The teacher's GeoGebra activities are paired with Poda, an AI tutor that follows the teacher's scaffolding protocol: students work in the applet, explain their thinking, and find the reasoning themselves. Teachers and researchers get a record of every turn.

The name comes from gorga, the carving on Batak traditional houses in North Sumatra, in three colours: red, white and black. Its patterns are full of symmetry, reflection and repetition. Mathematics has lived in it for a long time.

Poda is the Toba Batak word for advice given gently and indirectly. That is how the tutor works: it guides with questions instead of giving answers.
