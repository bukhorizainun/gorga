A conversation

The student finds zero alone.

Question: the temperature is −6 °C; how much must it rise to reach 4 °C? The student already answered 10 by counting one by one. The protocol does not let the tutor say “zero”. Watch how Gorga draws it out.

Studentso from -6 up to -5, then -4, then -3, -2, -1, then 0. Then up to 1, 2, 3, and finally 4.
GorgaYou went from -6 to -5, -4, -3, -2, -1, then to 1, 2, 3, up to 4. What happens as you pass a certain temperature before 1?L4
Studentoh, stop at zero first
GorgaYou stop at zero first. How do you count the rise from -6 to 0, and then from 0 to 4?L4
Studentfrom -6 to 0 it goes up 6
GorgaFrom -6 to 0 it goes up 6. Then from 0 to 4 it goes up by how much, and how does that give the total rise?L1
Studentthen 4 more, so 10
GorgaYour answer is correct. You split the rise into two parts: from -6 to 0 it rises 6 degrees, then from 0 to 4 it rises 4 degrees, 10 degrees in total.

“Correct” comes only at the end.

Counting one by one already gives 10, but it is not enough. Gorga confirms only after the student splits the change at 0 on their own, as the teacher's protocol requires.

This conversation ran in Indonesian in a simulation test and is translated here. Gorga's replies were written by the AI; the student lines were written by the team as test sentences.

The teacher's protocol

Help in steps, not answers.

The AI chooses its next move itself, but only up to the limit that applies at that moment. The longer a student is stuck, the more help it may give. The answer still has to come from the student.

L1 · Probe

Ask

Invites the student to try the applet and asks an open question.

“Try it with the thermometer. How did you get it?”
L4 · Revoice

Revoice

Repeats the student's idea in other words, then asks for an explanation.

“You counted 10 boxes. How did you count them?”
L2 · Point

Point

Directs attention to one thing on the thermometer.

“Look at 0. How many degrees from −6 to 0?”
L3 · Guided step

Break down

Breaks the question into small questions, without giving the result.

“From −6 to 0, how many? And from 0 to 4?”
Confirmation

Found

Given only after a correct answer and after the target reasoning comes from the student.

“You split at 0: 6, then 4, so 10.”
No give-aways

Every AI sentence is checked: numbers the student has not found yet may not appear.

Content, not labels

The check reads what the sentence says. An “L4” label that still splits the question is rejected.

Always a fallback

If the AI breaks a rule, the student gets a scripted sentence the teacher approved.

How it works

Three steps in class.

Nothing to install. Students open a link on a laptop or tablet.

1

Work in the applet

Move the markers in the GeoGebra applet, type an answer, press Check. Gorga reads the answer and the marker positions straight from the applet.

2

Explain your way

Gorga does not grade. It asks how the student got there, in the student's own everyday words.

3

Find it yourself

Step by step until the target reasoning appears. The path is drawn as motifs under the applet.

About the name

Gorga: an ornament you can compute.

Gorga is the carving on Batak traditional houses in North Sumatra, in three colours: red, white and black. Its patterns are full of symmetry, reflection and repetition. Mathematics has lived in it for a long time.

The Gorga logo is a simeol-eol scroll: one spiral r = aθ and its point reflection, with a band of ipon-ipon triangles below.

Gorga · a research prototype in mathematics education, non-commercial. Temperature Scale activity: Dr. Rahmi Ramadhani Umar. Thermometer applet: A.M. Vuković (2013), via GeoGebra.
